\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
This thesis presents a unified, species-agnostic pipeline for animal re-identification and population size estimation. The system combines global deep embeddings with local-feature aggregation (Fisher Vectors) and geometric verification for closed-set identification and for the Human-in-the-Loop Nested Importance Sampling (HITL-NIS) estimator for population counting. Across different benchmarks, the proposed approach matches (or even exceeds) best current methods for re-identification, while maintaining reasonable runtime. 

\subsection{Summary}
The main outcomes of the work are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A modular three-tier re-identification pipeline.} A retrieval
    -based system that can operate as (i) Global-only matching, (ii) Global+Fisher late fusion, or (iii) a full
     Global+Fisher+GV re-ranking pipeline (Chapter~\ref{chap:classification}).
    \item \textbf{A low-supervision calibration strategy for fusion.} Inspired by WildFusion, I used a small identity-based 
    calibration set to fit the Tier-2 fusion calibrators, reducing manual effort to on the order of \(\mathcal{O}(I^2)\) pair labels for 
    \(I\) chosen identities (Chapter~\ref{chap:classification}).
    \item \textbf{Population estimation with explicit human error modeling.} I extended HITL-NIS evaluation by simulating human annotation 
    mistakes and showing how confirmation strictness \(K\) maintains some level of robustness to realistic annotation noise (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key findings}
Two observations are particularly important for deployments:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy and efficiency can be balanced by limiting expensive local reasoning.} In the classification experiments, the Global+Fisher backbone already provides a strong baseline at low cost, while geometric verification can be reserved for final re-ranking when additional precision is needed (Table~\ref{tab:classification_results_final}).
    \item \textbf{Counting quality is dictated by re-identification quality and annotator reliability.} HITL-NIS operates on pair proposals derived from similarity signals so if the underlying re-ID is weak, the sampler wastes annotator queries on uninformative pairs and the estimator can become unstable or confidently wrong. This effect is most visible on ELPephants, the hardest dataset that was evaluated (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}). Even strong re-ID signals degrade quickly when annotator labels are noisy, and requiring repeated confirmation (\(K=2\)) is a simple and effective safeguard (Tables~\ref{tab:counting_error_sweep} and~\ref{tab:counting_error_sweep_k1}) (although it should be tested in realistic conditions with actual human annotators). These results are consistent with broader findings that identification errors can introduce systematic bias in camera-trap population studies~\cite{Johansson_Samelius_Wikberg_Chapron_Mishra_Low_2020}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}
\subsection{Broader benchmarking}

The most interesting extension of this work would be to collect a real life dataset of animals, maybe on an area where an approximate number of individuals is known by the local wildlife authorities. Something like this was attempted in the preparation for this paper by collecting camera-trap data from Nature Park Medvednica in Zagreb, but due to technical issues and not enough good quality images it was never completed and left for later date. The data that was collected can still be found on Kaggle\footnote{\url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/matejmaric/sljeme-trail-cams-processed-dataset/data}}

\subsection{Improving the global backbone and fusion}

The global embedding model is a critical component, it drives retrieval,
 affects shortlist quality, and strongly influences counting. Future work can improve generalization by 
 fine-tuning the global backbone on additional public re-ID datasets or custom camera-trap datasets. 

 \subsection{From Counting Photographed Individuals to True Abundance and Density}
 As evaluated in this thesis, the counting pipeline estimates the number of \emph{unique indiviudals present in the image collection}, not the actual number of individuals in an area. In many ecological applications, the target quantity is instead \emph{true abundance/density} in the study area. This requires modeling imperfect detection and sampling design. A promising direction seems to be to combine re-id with capture--recapture methods~\cite{https://doi.org/10.1890/12-0413.1}\cite{https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0030-1299.2004.13043.x} and author of this thesis certainly hopes to continue in that direction.
